**Quality Education in Resource-challenged Schools with the help of Technology**

**QuERST** (pronounced /kwerst/) is a multi-stakeholder, multi-component, and multi-phase framework and program aimed at leveraging information and communication technologies to rapidly improve the quality of learning of students in schools serving economically disadvantaged communities. At its core is **STutor** (Technology-Supported Student Tutoring), which is envisioned to provide tens of thousands of “learning-poor” Filipino students with willing, capable, and Lasallian values-inspired Filipino-speaking tutors from around the world, supported by AI-enabled apps for matching and modeling tutees and tutors. Initial findings from small-scale pilots suggest that online tutoring, when implemented with structured instructional protocols, can be effective in improving student outcomes—for example, incoming Grade 5 students who received tutoring last summer showed a statistically significant increase in mean test scores (p \= .012, effect size \= 0.9). Qualitative observations also revealed striking gains: students who previously relied on counting sticks to multiply two numbers were able to grasp the basics of place-value multiplication after just a single one-hour tutoring session. Without this intervention, it is unlikely they could manage the mathematical demands of Grade 5\. For STutor to scale effectively, it requires well-designed protocols for each step of the process—from tutor and tutee selection and matching all the way to final assessment and debriefing—as well as an integrated system, including the aforementioned apps, to facilitate protocol execution and support sustained stakeholder engagement. For more information, contact [raymund.sison@dlsu.edu.ph](http://raymund.sison@dlsu.edu.ph).

   
**Raymund Sison**

Raymund Sison is a University Fellow and Professor at De La Salle University (Philippines), where he has served as Dean of the College of Computer Studies and, more recently, Dean of the Brother Andrew Gonzalez College of Education. He is a recipient of the Metrobank Foundation’s Outstanding Teacher Award and the National Academy of Science and Technology’s Outstanding Young Scientist Award. He holds a PhD in Computer Science from the Tokyo Institute of Technology, with a focus on artificial intelligence and user modeling, and a PhD in Education from De La Salle University, specializing in educational leadership and grounded theory. He is the founding president of the Computing Society of the Philippines and the founder of **FORMDEV**, a volunteer community that facilitated lessons on the life and values of St. John Baptist de La Salle—an initiative that, though concluded, left a lasting impact on values formation at the university. Drawing from these diverse experiences, he conceptualized and spearheads **QuERST**, a framework and program that aims to leverage technology to improve the quality of learning in resource-challenged contexts.
